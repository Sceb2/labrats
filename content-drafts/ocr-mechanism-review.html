Organic mechanism practice

Four combined mechanisms and 16 focused questions, based on 16 marking groups in four OCR Chemistry A questions.

Original drawing practice, with teacher/self marking. This preview does not collect answers or calculate scores. It is separate from the revision tool’s question bank. Review the chemistry and marking before classroom use.

Full-headed curly arrows represent movement of an electron pair. Draw their tails at the electron source and their heads at the destination. Show formal and partial charges distinctly. A mark group containing several requirements earns one mark only when all requirements are met; no half marks. The focused questions repeat the same groups as the combined questions and do not constitute additional independent coverage.

MP-HCN-ALL · combined · 5 marks

HCN addition to methanal

Methanal, H2C=O, reacts with HCN in the presence of cyanide ions. Draw the complete mechanism, showing curly arrows, relevant dipoles and charges, the intermediate and the organic product. Name the mechanism. You may show protonation using H+ or water.

Draw your answer on paper or a separate canvas before revealing the guidance.

Show answer and marking guidance

M1 — Cyanide attack (1 mark)

An arrow from the carbon lone pair/negative charge of −C≡N to C of H2C=O.

Required for the mark

  • Cyanide attacks through carbon, not nitrogen.
  • The full-headed arrow starts at the carbon lone pair or negative charge on carbon and ends at the carbonyl carbon.

Common errors

  • Starting the arrow at nitrogen.
  • Starting at the carbonyl carbon and pointing to cyanide.
  • Pointing cyanide at oxygen.

Cyanide donates through carbon to the electron-deficient carbonyl carbon.

M2 — Carbonyl polarity and bond opening (1 mark)

H2Cδ+=Oδ− with an arrow from the C=O bond to O.

Required for the mark

  • Carbonyl carbon is δ+ and oxygen is δ−.
  • A full-headed curly arrow goes from the C=O bond to oxygen.

Common errors

  • Correct arrow but missing or reversed dipole.
  • Arrow from O into the C=O bond.
  • Formal +/− charges used on the neutral starting carbonyl in place of δ+/δ−.

The π pair moves to oxygen. Show the starting dipole as well; this is one combined mark.

M3 — Intermediate and protonation (1 mark)

−O–CH2–C≡N; arrow from O− to H+. Alternatively, arrow from its O lone pair to H of H2O; a complete water pathway also shows that water O–H bond's electrons returning to water O, producing OH−.

Required for the mark

  • Correct alkoxide intermediate: −O–CH2–C≡N, with a single C–O bond and negative charge on O.
  • A full-headed arrow starts at the O lone pair or O− charge and ends at H+ or a hydrogen of water.

Common errors

  • Correct protonation arrow drawn on an incorrect intermediate.
  • Oδ− substituted for the intermediate's formal O− charge.
  • Arrow from H+ towards oxygen.
  • Cyanide bonded through nitrogen.

The intermediate is an alkoxide. Its negatively charged oxygen donates a pair to a proton.

M4 — Hydroxynitrile product (1 mark)

HO–CH2–C≡N (2-hydroxyethanenitrile). A correct unambiguous structure is sufficient; its name is not an extra requirement.

Required for the mark

  • HO–CH2–C≡N, with the new C–C bond and OH on the former carbonyl carbon.

Common errors

  • An N-bonded isocyanide product.
  • Leaving a C=O bond in the addition product.
  • Omitting the OH group or the added carbon.

Addition forms a hydroxynitrile and adds one carbon to the original carbonyl skeleton.

M5 — Mechanism name (1 mark)

Nucleophilic addition.

Required for the mark

  • Nucleophilic addition.

Common errors

  • Nucleophilic substitution.
  • Electrophilic addition.

CN− is a nucleophile and adds across the carbonyl; no group is substituted.

Source and adaptation

Original practice inspired by 2020 autumn: paper dated 13 October; scheme labelled November, H432/02, Q19(c)(i). Not an OCR question.

Question printed p. 19 / PDF p. 19; scheme printed pp. 16 / PDF pp. 17.

Methanal replaces the source's branched aldehyde. It preserves the five marking groups while avoiding stereochemical and alkyl-chain distractions.

  • Carbonyl dipole and C=O-to-O arrow earn one mark together.
  • The protonation mark requires the correct alkoxide intermediate as well as the arrow.
  • Cyanide attacks through carbon. The arrow can start at its carbon lone pair or the negative charge on carbon.
  • For protonation, attack on H+ or on a hydrogen of water is allowed. The source ignores an attempted water O-H bond-breaking arrow and a missing water dipole. A complete water pathway is shown in the original answer guidance.

MP-HBR-ALL · combined · 4 marks

HBr addition to 2-methylpropene

2-Methylpropene, CH2=C(CH3)2, reacts with hydrogen bromide by electrophilic addition. Draw the mechanism for the major product. Include curly arrows, the H–Br dipole, the charged intermediate and the organic product.

Draw your answer on paper or a separate canvas before revealing the guidance.

Show answer and marking guidance

M1 — Alkene attack on H (1 mark)

Arrow from the C=C π bond to H of H–Br.

Required for the mark

  • A full-headed arrow starts at the C=C bond and ends at H of H–Br.
  • Do not add a partial charge to the starting C=C bond.

Common errors

  • Arrow from H to C=C.
  • Arrow to Br rather than H.
  • Starting at one carbon atom instead of the electron pair in the bond.

The alkene π pair is the electron source; hydrogen is the electrophilic destination.

M2 — HBr polarity and bond breaking (1 mark)

Hδ+–Brδ−, with the bond electron pair moving to Br to form Br−.

Required for the mark

  • Hδ+–Brδ−.
  • A full-headed arrow from the H–Br bond to Br.

Common errors

  • Correct dipole without the arrow.
  • Reversed dipole.
  • Arrow to H, producing H−.

Br takes the H–Br bond pair. The dipole and arrow form one marking group.

M3 — Major-route carbocation and Br− attack (1 mark)

(CH3)3C+ plus Br−; arrow from a Br− electron pair/negative charge to the positively charged central carbon.

Required for the mark

  • The tertiary carbocation (CH3)3C+ is drawn with a formal + charge at the central carbon.
  • A full-headed arrow starts at a Br− lone pair or its negative charge and ends at that C+.

Common errors

  • Primary (CH3)2CH–CH2+ used for the major-product route.
  • δ+ instead of + on the carbocation.
  • Arrow from C+ to Br−.
  • Correct arrow drawn on an incorrect intermediate.

The major route forms the more stable tertiary carbocation. Bromide donates the pair that makes the new C–Br bond.

M4 — Major organic product (1 mark)

2-Bromo-2-methylpropane, (CH3)3C–Br.

Required for the mark

  • (CH3)3C–Br, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.

Teacher judgement: For the combined drawing task, a teacher may award this product group by error carried forward if the student drew the other carbocation and the product follows correctly from it. M3 is still lost for the wrong major-route intermediate. In this independent focused task no earlier intermediate is supplied, so require the stated major product.

Common errors

  • 1-Bromo-2-methylpropane given as the major product.
  • A dibromoalkane, as though the reagent were Br2.

Br is bonded to the carbon that carried the positive charge in the major-route intermediate.

Source and adaptation

Original practice inspired by October 2021, H432/02, Q16(b)(i). Not an OCR question.

Question printed p. 10 / PDF p. 10; scheme printed pp. 5, 6 / PDF pp. 6, 7.

2-Methylpropene replaces but-1-ene. The intended route forms a tertiary rather than secondary carbocation; this changes the substrate without changing the four assessed groups. This is ordinary electrophilic addition, not peroxide-initiated radical addition.

  • H-Br dipole and H-Br bond-breaking arrow earn one mark together.
  • The correct carbocation and Br− attack arrow earn one mark together; δ+ does not replace a formal + charge.
  • The source explicitly allows error carried forward for a product from an incorrect carbocation. This needs human judgement; do not silently replace it with exact-answer-only exam marking.
  • Do not place a partial charge on the starting C=C bond. The source refers use of Br2 instead of HBr to a team leader.

MP-BR2-ALL · combined · 3 marks

Bromine addition to ethene

Ethene reacts with bromine. Using the open-chain carbocation representation for this exercise, draw the electrophilic addition mechanism. Include the induced dipole on Br2, all electron-pair arrows, the charged intermediate and the organic product.

Draw your answer on paper or a separate canvas before revealing the guidance.

Show answer and marking guidance

M1 — Induced dipole and two first-stage arrows (1 mark)

Brδ+–Brδ−: C=C → Brδ+ and Br–Br bond → Brδ−. A left/right mirror orientation is equally valid if all labels and arrows remain consistent.

Required for the mark

  • A correctly oriented Brδ+–Brδ− induced dipole.
  • A full-headed arrow from the C=C bond to the Brδ+ atom.
  • A full-headed arrow from the Br–Br bond to Brδ−.
  • Do not put a partial charge on the starting C=C bond.

Common errors

  • Only one of the two arrows shown.
  • Both arrows correct but dipole missing.
  • Arrow to Brδ− from the alkene.
  • Treating this group as three marks.

The alkene polarises bromine. The dipole and both first-stage arrows together earn one mark.

M2 — Intermediate and bromide attack (1 mark)

BrCH2–CH2+ plus Br−; arrow from Br− to the positive carbon. The reflected intermediate +CH2–CH2Br is equivalent.

Required for the mark

  • BrCH2–CH2+ (or the reflected equivalent), with one attached Br and one formally positive carbon.
  • A full-headed arrow from a Br− lone pair/negative charge to C+.

Common errors

  • No attached Br on the intermediate.
  • δ+ instead of a formal + charge.
  • Arrow drawn from carbon to bromide.

The first Br is already bonded. The second Br comes from bromide donating an electron pair to the carbocation.

M3 — Dibromoalkane product (1 mark)

BrCH2–CH2Br, with one Br on each former alkene carbon.

Required for the mark

  • BrCH2–CH2Br: 1,2-dibromoethane.

Common errors

  • Bromoethane, as though the reagent were HBr.
  • Both Br atoms placed on the same carbon.
  • Leaving the C=C bond in the product.

The double bond is replaced by a C–C single bond and one C–Br bond at each carbon.

Source and adaptation

Original practice inspired by June 2022, H432/02, Q16(a)(ii). Not an OCR question.

Question printed p. 10 / PDF p. 10; scheme printed pp. 10, 11, 12 / PDF pp. 11, 12, 13.

Ethene replaces 3-methylhex-2-ene. It preserves the three marking groups but removes regioisomer and stereochemistry choices. Use the specified open-chain carbocation representation. Bromonium mechanisms are chemically valid and are accepted with referral in the original scheme; they are not distractors here.

  • The induced Br-Br dipole and BOTH first-stage arrows earn ONE mark together.
  • The correct carbocation consistent with the mechanism and Br− attack earn one mark together. Either original alkene carbon can carry the positive charge in the source.
  • The third mark is for the product matching the mechanism; its guidance continues onto printed page 12 (PDF page 13). Do not import an identical explicit carbocation ECF rule from the 2021 scheme.
  • A bromonium route is allowed with team-leader referral in the source. The original exercises deliberately specify the open-chain carbocation representation; a bromonium mechanism must not be presented as chemically wrong.
  • The source disallows HBr in place of Br2 but allows ECF for subsequent use. It disallows partial charge on C=C. Its arrow guidance includes examiner-specific ECF; the draft is not a replica of those examiner procedures.

Catalysed chlorination of benzene

Benzene reacts with Cl2 in the presence of AlCl3. Draw the electrophilic substitution mechanism, including the intermediate, charges and curly arrows. Give equations showing formation of the electrophile and regeneration of the catalyst. Show the organic product.

Draw your answer on paper or a separate canvas before revealing the guidance.

Show answer and marking guidance

M1 — Electrophile generation and catalyst regeneration (1 mark)

Cl2 + AlCl3 → Cl+ + AlCl4−; H+ + AlCl4− → AlCl3 + HCl. Alternative pair: Cl2 + FeCl3 → Cl+ + FeCl4−; H+ + FeCl4− → FeCl3 + HCl.

Required for the mark

  • Cl2 + AlCl3 → Cl+ + AlCl4−.
  • H+ + AlCl4− → AlCl3 + HCl.
  • Alternatively, both corresponding FeCl3/FeCl4− equations are correct. Do not mix aluminium and iron species between the pair.

Common errors

  • Giving only electrophile formation.
  • Cl− instead of Cl+.
  • Not regenerating the catalyst.
  • Treating the two equations as two marks.

The halogen carrier generates the electrophile and is regenerated. Both equations together earn one mark.

M2 — π-system attack (1 mark)

Curly arrow from the inner π-circle to Cl+.

Required for the mark

  • A full-headed arrow starts at the benzene π-system and ends at Cl+.

Common errors

  • Arrow from Cl+ to the ring.
  • Arrow from a C–H bond.
  • Cl− used as the attacking species.

The arene π electrons donate to the electrophile; the positive chlorine is the destination, not the source.

M3 — Aromatic intermediate (1 mark)

A six-membered ring with H and Cl on the attacked carbon, an inner partial circle extending over more than half the ring with a gap towards that carbon, and + inside the hexagon.

Required for the mark

  • H and Cl are attached to the same, attacked ring carbon.
  • The inner partial π-ring extends over more than half the hexagon, with its gap facing that carbon.
  • A formal + sign appears inside the ring; a full aromatic circle is absent.

Common errors

  • A complete aromatic circle retained after addition.
  • Missing + or using δ+.
  • A partial-circle gap facing the wrong carbon.
  • Removing H before drawing the intermediate.

Attack temporarily breaks aromaticity. The attacked carbon retains H and gains Cl; the positive charge is delocalised over the remaining π-system.

M4 — Restoring the aromatic ring (1 mark)

Curly arrow from the C–H bond at the Cl-bearing carbon into the ring. H+ is lost and chlorobenzene forms. Product is shown for completeness, with no extra mark allocated.

Required for the mark

  • A full-headed arrow starts at the C–H bond on the carbon bearing Cl and ends inside the ring to restore the π-system.

Common errors

  • Arrow beginning on H rather than the C–H bond.
  • Arrow taking the C–Cl bond pair back into the ring.
  • Arrow from the ring towards H.

The C–H bond pair returns to the π-system as H+ is lost; the C–Cl bond remains.
