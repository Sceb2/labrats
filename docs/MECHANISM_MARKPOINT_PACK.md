### 2020 autumn: paper dated 13 October; scheme labelled November, H432/02, Q19(c)(i) — 5 marks

Source reaction: HCN addition to the supplied branched aldehyde F.

Question paper: `643572-question-paper-synthesis-and-analytical-techniques.pdf`, printed p. 19 (PDF p. 19). Mark scheme: `643577-mark-scheme-synthesis-and-analytical-techniques.pdf`, printed pp. 16 (PDF pp. 17).

- **M1 (1 mark):** Arrow from cyanide's carbon electron pair/negative charge to the carbonyl carbon.
- **M2 (1 mark):** Correct carbonyl dipole AND C=O-to-O arrow together.
- **M3 (1 mark):** Correct alkoxide intermediate AND O-to-proton arrow together.
- **M4 (1 mark):** Correct organic product.
- **M5 (1 mark):** Nucleophilic addition.

**Conditions that affect interpretation:**

- Carbonyl dipole and C=O-to-O arrow earn one mark together.
- The protonation mark requires the correct alkoxide intermediate as well as the arrow.
- Cyanide attacks through carbon. The arrow can start at its carbon lone pair or the negative charge on carbon.
- For protonation, attack on H+ or on a hydrogen of water is allowed. The source ignores an attempted water O-H bond-breaking arrow and a missing water dipole. A complete water pathway is shown in the original answer guidance.

**Original-question adaptation:** Methanal replaces the source's branched aldehyde. It preserves the five marking groups while avoiding stereochemical and alkyl-chain distractions.

### October 2021, H432/02, Q16(b)(i) — 4 marks

## Original questions and answer guidance

Full-headed curly arrows represent movement of an electron pair. Draw their tails at the electron source and their heads at the destination. Show formal and partial charges distinctly. A mark group containing several requirements earns one mark only when all requirements are met; no half marks. The focused questions repeat the same groups as the combined questions and do not constitute additional independent coverage.

Use the prompts without the answer guidance for an unaided attempt. Focused questions assess a complete marking group: when a group contains two or three linked features, they remain one mark. No extra point is awarded for an unrequested mechanism name or stability explanation.

### HCN addition to methanal

Reaction: H2C=O + HCN → HOCH2C≡N

**Asset brief:** Draw methanal with its C=O bond clear and show a separate cyanide ion with unambiguous carbon and nitrogen labels. The student view must not pre-fill the carbonyl dipole, attack arrow, intermediate or product. Provide separate, uncrossed drawing areas for attack and protonation.

#### MP-HCN-ALL — Combined mechanism [5 marks]

**Student prompt:** Methanal, H2C=O, reacts with HCN in the presence of cyanide ions. Draw the complete mechanism, showing curly arrows, relevant dipoles and charges, the intermediate and the organic product. Name the mechanism. You may show protonation using H+ or water.

**Answer and marking:**

- **M1, 1 mark:** An arrow from the carbon lone pair/negative charge of −C≡N to C of H2C=O.
  Required together: Cyanide attacks through carbon, not nitrogen. The full-headed arrow starts at the carbon lone pair or negative charge on carbon and ends at the carbonyl carbon.
- **M2, 1 mark:** H2Cδ+=Oδ− with an arrow from the C=O bond to O.
  Required together: Carbonyl carbon is δ+ and oxygen is δ−. A full-headed curly arrow goes from the C=O bond to oxygen.
- **M3, 1 mark:** −O–CH2–C≡N; arrow from O− to H+. Alternatively, arrow from its O lone pair to H of H2O; a complete water pathway also shows that water O–H bond's electrons returning to water O, producing OH−.
  Required together: Correct alkoxide intermediate: −O–CH2–C≡N, with a single C–O bond and negative charge on O. A full-headed arrow starts at the O lone pair or O− charge and ends at H+ or a hydrogen of water.
- **M4, 1 mark:** HO–CH2–C≡N (2-hydroxyethanenitrile). A correct unambiguous structure is sufficient; its name is not an extra requirement.
- **M5, 1 mark:** Nucleophilic addition.

**Common errors / feedback:**

- **M1:** Starting the arrow at nitrogen. Starting at the carbonyl carbon and pointing to cyanide. Pointing cyanide at oxygen. Cyanide donates through carbon to the electron-deficient carbonyl carbon.
- **M2:** Correct arrow but missing or reversed dipole. Arrow from O into the C=O bond. Formal +/− charges used on the neutral starting carbonyl in place of δ+/δ−. The π pair moves to oxygen. Show the starting dipole as well; this is one combined mark.
- **M3:** Correct protonation arrow drawn on an incorrect intermediate. Oδ− substituted for the intermediate's formal O− charge. Arrow from H+ towards oxygen. Cyanide bonded through nitrogen. The intermediate is an alkoxide. Its negatively charged oxygen donates a pair to a proton.
- **M4:** An N-bonded isocyanide product. Leaving a C=O bond in the addition product. Omitting the OH group or the added carbon. Addition forms a hydroxynitrile and adds one carbon to the original carbonyl skeleton.
- **M5:** Nucleophilic substitution. Electrophilic addition. CN− is a nucleophile and adds across the carbonyl; no group is substituted.

### HBr addition to 2-methylpropene

Reaction: CH2=C(CH3)2 + HBr → (CH3)3C–Br (major product)

**Asset brief:** Show CH2=C(CH3)2 with the double bond unobstructed, and separate H–Br. Leave the dipole and all arrows blank. Keep the subsequent carbocation and nucleophile in a separate stage with room for a clear Br−-to-carbon arrow.

#### MP-HBR-ALL — Combined mechanism [4 marks]

**Student prompt:** 2-Methylpropene, CH2=C(CH3)2, reacts with hydrogen bromide by electrophilic addition. Draw the mechanism for the major product. Include curly arrows, the H–Br dipole, the charged intermediate and the organic product.

**Answer and marking:**

- **M1, 1 mark:** Arrow from the C=C π bond to H of H–Br.
  Required together: A full-headed arrow starts at the C=C bond and ends at H of H–Br. Do not add a partial charge to the starting C=C bond.
- **M2, 1 mark:** Hδ+–Brδ−, with the bond electron pair moving to Br to form Br−.
  Required together: Hδ+–Brδ−. A full-headed arrow from the H–Br bond to Br.
- **M3, 1 mark:** (CH3)3C+ plus Br−; arrow from a Br− electron pair/negative charge to the positively charged central carbon.
  Required together: The tertiary carbocation (CH3)3C+ is drawn with a formal + charge at the central carbon. A full-headed arrow starts at a Br− lone pair or its negative charge and ends at that C+.
- **M4, 1 mark:** 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane, (CH3)3C–Br.
  Teacher judgement: For the combined drawing task, a teacher may award this product group by error carried forward if the student drew the other carbocation and the product follows correctly from it. M3 is still lost for the wrong major-route intermediate. In this independent focused task no earlier intermediate is supplied, so require the stated major product.

**Common errors / feedback:**

- **M1:** Arrow from H to C=C. Arrow to Br rather than H. Starting at one carbon atom instead of the electron pair in the bond. The alkene π pair is the electron source; hydrogen is the electrophilic destination.
- **M2:** Correct dipole without the arrow. Reversed dipole. Arrow to H, producing H−. Br takes the H–Br bond pair. The dipole and arrow form one marking group.
- **M3:** Primary (CH3)2CH–CH2+ used for the major-product route. δ+ instead of + on the carbocation. Arrow from C+ to Br−. Correct arrow drawn on an incorrect intermediate. The major route forms the more stable tertiary carbocation. Bromide donates the pair that makes the new C–Br bond.
- **M4:** 1-Bromo-2-methylpropane given as the major product. A dibromoalkane, as though the reagent were Br2. Br is bonded to the carbon that carried the positive charge in the major-route intermediate.

### Bromine addition to ethene

Reaction: CH2=CH2 + Br2 → BrCH2–CH2Br

**Asset brief:** Show ethene and a separate Br–Br bond, with neither a dipole nor any arrows pre-filled. Allow either orientation of the induced dipole when the arrow destinations agree. Draw the intermediate in a separate area. Do not place δ charges on ethene.

#### MP-BR2-ALL — Combined mechanism [3 marks]

**Student prompt:** Ethene reacts with bromine. Using the open-chain carbocation representation for this exercise, draw the electrophilic addition mechanism. Include the induced dipole on Br2, all electron-pair arrows, the charged intermediate and the organic product.

**Answer and marking:**

- **M1, 1 mark:** Brδ+–Brδ−: C=C → Brδ+ and Br–Br bond → Brδ−. A left/right mirror orientation is equally valid if all labels and arrows remain consistent.
  Required together: A correctly oriented Brδ+–Brδ− induced dipole. A full-headed arrow from the C=C bond to the Brδ+ atom. A full-headed arrow from the Br–Br bond to Brδ−. Do not put a partial charge on the starting C=C bond.
- **M2, 1 mark:** BrCH2–CH2+ plus Br−; arrow from Br− to the positive carbon. The reflected intermediate +CH2–CH2Br is equivalent.
  Required together: BrCH2–CH2+ (or the reflected equivalent), with one attached Br and one formally positive carbon. A full-headed arrow from a Br− lone pair/negative charge to C+.
- **M3, 1 mark:** BrCH2–CH2Br, with one Br on each former alkene carbon.

**Common errors / feedback:**

- **M1:** Only one of the two arrows shown. Both arrows correct but dipole missing. Arrow to Brδ− from the alkene. Treating this group as three marks. The alkene polarises bromine. The dipole and both first-stage arrows together earn one mark.
- **M2:** No attached Br on the intermediate. δ+ instead of a formal + charge. Arrow drawn from carbon to bromide. The first Br is already bonded. The second Br comes from bromide donating an electron pair to the carbocation.
- **M3:** Bromoethane, as though the reagent were HBr. Both Br atoms placed on the same carbon. Leaving the C=C bond in the product. The double bond is replaced by a C–C single bond and one C–Br bond at each carbon.

### Catalysed chlorination of benzene

Reaction: C6H6 + Cl2 → C6H5Cl + HCl; AlCl3 catalyst

**Asset brief:** Use a large benzene hexagon and inner circle. Place the electrophile away from any ring labels. Leave a separate intermediate area with room to show H and Cl on the same carbon, the partial π-ring, positive charge and C–H arrow without crossing labels. Product is required for a complete response but is not an extra fifth mark.

#### MP-EAS-ALL — Combined mechanism [4 marks]

**Student prompt:** Benzene reacts with Cl2 in the presence of AlCl3. Draw the electrophilic substitution mechanism, including the intermediate, charges and curly arrows. Give equations showing formation of the electrophile and regeneration of the catalyst. Show the organic product.

**Answer and marking:**

- **M1, 1 mark:** Cl2 + AlCl3 → Cl+ + AlCl4−; H+ + AlCl4− → AlCl3 + HCl. Alternative pair: Cl2 + FeCl3 → Cl+ + FeCl4−; H+ + FeCl4− → FeCl3 + HCl.
  Required together: Cl2 + AlCl3 → Cl+ + AlCl4−. H+ + AlCl4− → AlCl3 + HCl. Alternatively, both corresponding FeCl3/FeCl4− equations are correct. Do not mix aluminium and iron species between the pair.
- **M2, 1 mark:** Curly arrow from the inner π-circle to Cl+.
- **M3, 1 mark:** A six-membered ring with H and Cl on the attacked carbon, an inner partial circle extending over more than half the ring with a gap towards that carbon, and + inside the hexagon.
  Required together: H and Cl are attached to the same, attacked ring carbon. The inner partial π-ring extends over more than half the hexagon, with its gap facing that carbon. A formal + sign appears inside the ring; a full aromatic circle is absent.
- **M4, 1 mark:** Curly arrow from the C–H bond at the Cl-bearing carbon into the ring. H+ is lost and chlorobenzene forms. Product is shown for completeness, with no extra mark allocated.

**Common errors / feedback:**

- **M1:** Giving only electrophile formation. Cl− instead of Cl+. Not regenerating the catalyst. Treating the two equations as two marks. The halogen carrier generates the electrophile and is regenerated. Both equations together earn one mark.
- **M2:** Arrow from Cl+ to the ring. Arrow from a C–H bond. Cl− used as the attacking species. The arene π electrons donate to the electrophile; the positive chlorine is the destination, not the source.
- **M3:** A complete aromatic circle retained after addition. Missing + or using δ+. A partial-circle gap facing the wrong carbon. Removing H before drawing the intermediate. Attack temporarily breaks aromaticity. The attacked carbon retains H and gains Cl; the positive charge is delocalised over the remaining π-system.
- **M4:** Arrow beginning on H rather than the C–H bond. Arrow taking the C–Cl bond pair back into the ring. Arrow from the ring towards H. The C–H bond pair returns to the π-system as H+ is lost; the C–Cl bond remains.
