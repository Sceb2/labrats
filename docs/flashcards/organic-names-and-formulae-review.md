# Organic names and formulae: OCR-aligned draft

33 cards. Formulae use subscripts and condensed structural notation following OCR Chemistry A. See OCR_FORMULA_REVIEW.md for the source references.

## 1. Which homologous series has the functional group -COOH?

Carboxylic acids.

## 2. What is the functional group of an alcohol?

An -OH group bonded to a saturated carbon atom.

## 3. Which homologous series contains a carbon-nitrogen triple bond?

Nitriles. The carbon atom of the nitrile group is bonded to the rest of the molecule.

## 4. Which class of compounds includes molecules with an -NH₂ group bonded to an alkyl group?

Primary amines.

## 5. What is the name of the -CH₃ substituent?

Methyl.

## 6. What is the structural formula of propane?

CH₃CH₂CH₃

## 7. What is the molecular formula of hex-1-ene?

C₆H₁₂

## 8. What suffix is used when naming a carboxylic acid?

The suffix is -oic acid, as in ethanoic acid.

## 9. What ending is used for the acid-derived part of an ester name?

The ending is -oate, as in ethyl ethanoate.

## 10. What suffix and prefix are used to name an -OH group?

The suffix is -ol, as in ethanol.
The prefix is hydroxy- when a higher-priority functional group determines the suffix.

## 11. How is an ester written in structural formula notation using R groups?

RCOOR′
R and R′ represent organic groups that may be different.

## 12. What class of compounds has the structure R–O–R′?

Ethers.

## 13. What is the structural difference between an aldehyde and a ketone?

An aldehyde has a carbonyl carbon bonded to at least one H atom: -CHO.
A ketone has a carbonyl carbon bonded to two carbon atoms.

## 14. What is the functional group of an alkene?

A carbon-carbon double bond, C=C.

## 15. What type of carbon-carbon bonds are present in an alkane?

Single covalent bonds only.

## 16. What group is present in both aldehydes and ketones?

The carbonyl group, C=O.

## 17. What suffix is used when naming a ketone?

The suffix is -one, as in propanone.

## 18. What suffix is used when naming an aldehyde?

The suffix is -al, as in ethanal.

## 19. What is a functional group?

An atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic chemical reactions of an organic compound.

## 20. What is a homologous series?

A series of organic compounds having the same functional group, with each successive member differing by CH₂.

## 21. What does a displayed formula show?

The relative positioning of atoms and the bonds between them.
Every atom and every bond is shown.

## 22. How do you choose the numbering direction when naming a simple branched alkane?

Number the parent chain to give the branches the lowest possible set of numbers.
Compare the sets at the first point where they differ.

## 23. How do you choose the parent chain of a simple branched alkane?

Choose the longest continuous carbon chain.
The number of carbon atoms in that chain determines the stem of the name.

## 24. What does R represent in an organic formula?

An alkyl group or another fragment of an organic compound that is not involved in the reaction.

## 25. What is an alkyl group?

A group formed by removing one H atom from an alkane.
For example, methane gives the methyl group, -CH₃.

## 26. What is a haloalkane?

A compound formed when one or more H atoms in an alkane are replaced by halogen atoms.

## 27. Which homologous series has the functional group –COCl?

Acyl chlorides.

## 28. What is an alkane?

A saturated, acyclic hydrocarbon.
It contains only carbon and hydrogen, with single bonds only.

## 29. What ending is used when naming an acyl chloride?

The ending is -oyl chloride, as in ethanoyl chloride.

## 30. What is a general formula?

The simplest algebraic formula of a member of a homologous series.
For alkanes: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

## 31. What is an empirical formula?

The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.

## 32. What is a molecular formula?

The actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule.

## 33. What does a structural formula show?

The minimal detail that shows the arrangement of atoms in a molecule.
For example, CH₃CH₂OH is the structural formula of ethanol.
